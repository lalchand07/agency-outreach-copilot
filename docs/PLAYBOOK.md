# Outreach playbook: selling software to travel agencies

A practical guide for a small software company selling a back-office product (bookings, package costing, invoicing, supplier payments, accounts) to travel agencies in the UK, Pakistan and the Gulf. Adapt it to your own product.

The short version: **pick a narrow segment, write fewer and better messages, offer something small and useful, follow up once, and make it effortless to say no.**

---

## 1. Who to target

### Best-fit agencies

| Signal | Why it matters | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|
| Sells **packages** (Umrah, Hajj, holidays), not just tickets | Packages mean costing, multiple suppliers and part payments: the pain a back office solves | Package pages, "from £…" prices |
| **2–50 staff** | Big enough to feel spreadsheet chaos, small enough to decide quickly | "Our team", branch count, staff photos |
| **Static prices** or "call for a quote" | Pricing and bookings are probably managed by hand | No live search widget; enquiry forms |
| **ATOL / IATA / ABTA** (UK), **IATA / DTS** (Pakistan), **DTCM** (Dubai) | Established and regulated; record-keeping matters to them | Footer logos, "ATOL protected", licence numbers |
| **WhatsApp** as the main sales channel | Fast-moving, owner-led businesses; often receptive to WhatsApp follow-up | wa.me links, "chat with us" |
| Several **branches** or **sub-agents** | Consolidated reporting and agent statements | Branch pages, "agents wanted" |

### Usually a poor fit (for now)

- Large OTAs and consolidators with in-house tech.
- One-person agencies selling only flights on commission (little back-office work), unless your product has a starter plan.
- Agencies with an obvious modern booking platform already (live search, online payments, customer accounts).

### Segments that work well as campaigns

- **UK Umrah & Hajj specialists** in Birmingham, London (East/North), Manchester, Bradford, Leicester, Glasgow. Many are family-run, ATOL-protected and busy around Ramadan and the Hajj season.
- **Pakistan Hajj/Umrah operators and travel agencies** in Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad/Rawalpindi, Faisalabad, Peshawar. Urdu messages often get more replies than English ones; the owner usually reads WhatsApp first.
- **Gulf agencies serving South Asian expatriates** in Dubai, Sharjah, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Jeddah, Doha. English or Arabic, often also Urdu/Hindi-speaking staff.
- **Trade show exhibitors**: after a travel trade event, the exhibitor list (where its terms allow) gives you a relevant, current set of agencies and a natural opener ("we were both at …").

**Check first:** in the UK, sole traders and partnerships are treated like individuals for email marketing rules. See the README's compliance section before emailing them.

## 2. Before you send anything

- **Have one clear offer.** "A free 20-minute demo using your own packages, and we import your current bookings for free." Small, concrete, low risk.
- **Have proof.** A 90-second screen recording of creating an Umrah package (flights + hotels Makkah/Madinah + visa + transport) and generating the invoice. A GIF in the email is unnecessary; a link in the follow-up or the WhatsApp message is enough.
- **Set up your mailbox properly.** Use a real person's mailbox on a domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Consider a separate sending subdomain. Start with 10–20 emails a day.
- **Know the calendar.** Agencies are flat out before Ramadan, during the Hajj booking window and before school holidays. Reach out in quieter weeks and pitch "get ready before the season".

## 3. Message examples

Every message: one specific, true observation from their site, why it matters, the offer, an easy question. No flattery, no fake familiarity, no invented numbers.

### Email (English, ≤ 120 words)

> **Subject:** Costing your Umrah packages
>
> Hello Al-Noor Travel team,
>
> Your site lists 10- and 14-night Umrah packages with a choice of 3-, 4- and 5-star hotels. Keeping hotel, visa and flight costs right for every option is a lot of spreadsheet work.
>
> We make TBM, a back office for travel agencies: package costing, invoices, customer payments and supplier balances in one place.
>
> Could I show you in 20 minutes, using one of your own packages? We also import your existing bookings for free.
>
> Thanks,
> Sam

### Email (Urdu, ≤ 120 words)

> **Subject:** آپ کے عمرہ پیکجز کی کاسٹنگ
>
> السلام علیکم،
>
> آپ کی ویب سائٹ پر 3، 4 اور 5 اسٹار ہوٹلوں کے ساتھ عمرہ پیکجز موجود ہیں۔ ہر پیکج کے لیے ہوٹل، ویزا اور ٹکٹ کی لاگت کا حساب رکھنا کافی محنت کا کام ہے۔
>
> ہم TBM بناتے ہیں، ٹریول ایجنسیوں کے لیے بیک آفس سسٹم: پیکج کاسٹنگ، انوائس، کسٹمر ادائیگیاں اور سپلائر بیلنس ایک جگہ۔
>
> کیا میں آپ کے اپنے ایک پیکج کے ساتھ 20 منٹ کا مفت ڈیمو دکھا سکتا ہوں؟ آپ کا موجودہ ڈیٹا بھی ہم مفت منتقل کر دیں گے۔
>
> شکریہ،
> سام

(Have a native speaker check every non-English draft.)

### LinkedIn connection note (≤ 300 characters)

> Hi Ahmed, I saw Al-Noor Travel runs Umrah packages from Birmingham. I build back-office software for travel agencies (package costing, invoices, supplier payments) and would like to connect with people running agencies in the UK.

### WhatsApp (≤ 60 words, sent by hand)

> Assalamu alaikum, this is Sam from Seller Ltd. I saw your Umrah packages on your website. We make a back-office system for travel agencies (package costing, invoices, supplier payments). Would a free 20-minute demo be useful? If not, no problem at all and I won't message again.

WhatsApp is personal. Only message a business number that the agency publishes for enquiries, send one message, and stop if there is no answer.

### Follow-up (one only, ≤ 80 words, after 5–7 days)

> **Subject:** Re: Costing your Umrah packages
>
> Hello again,
>
> Just making sure my note reached you. If keeping package costs and supplier payments in one place would help before the next season, I'm happy to do a 20-minute demo with one of your packages.
>
> If it's not relevant, just say so and I won't write again.
>
> Sam

### What not to write

- "As discussed…", "Following up on our call…" (you never spoke).
- "I loved your website!" (fake familiarity).
- "Increase your profits by 300%", "the #1 system" (unprovable).
- "Last chance", "only 3 spots left" (pressure).
- Long feature lists. One benefit that matches the observation is enough.

The app flags these phrases in the review queue.

## 4. Follow-up timing

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | First email (Tuesday–Thursday, 9–11 am local time works well) |
| 0–2 | Optional LinkedIn connection note to the owner/manager, if you can find them |
| 5–7 | **One** follow-up email, shorter, with an easy "no" |
| 7–10 | Optional single WhatsApp message to the published business number, if that is how they sell |
| After that | Stop. Set a next-action date 3–6 months out if they are a strong fit and did not say no |

If someone says "not now", ask when would be better, set the next-action date, and do not follow up before it. If someone says "no" or "remove me", set **Do not contact**: the app suppresses their email, domain and phone.

## 5. Demo script outline (20 minutes)

1. **Two questions first (3 min).** "How do you cost a package today?" "Where do you track what customers have paid and what you owe suppliers?" Listen; write down their words.
2. **Their package, not a generic one (7 min).** Build one of the packages from their own website: flights, Makkah and Madinah hotels, visa, transport, margin. Show the selling price per person and per room type.
3. **From booking to money (5 min).** Turn it into a booking for a family, take a deposit, issue the invoice/receipt, show the customer balance and the supplier payable. If they are ATOL-protected, show where the records live.
4. **The month-end view (2 min).** Profit per booking/package, outstanding customer balances, supplier statements.
5. **Next step (3 min).** Offer the free data import and a two-week trial with their real data. Agree a date. Ask who else needs to see it (partner, accountant).

Afterwards: send a short summary email the same day with the recording link (if they agreed to recording), the price, and the agreed next step. Update the lead to **Demo booked → Trial** in the pipeline.

---

## 6. Measuring

Watch the dashboard weekly:

- **Reply rate** (any reply, including "no"): below 5% means the targeting or the first line is off. Change one thing at a time.
- **Positive reply → demo**: if people reply but do not book, make the offer smaller or more concrete.
- **Unsubscribes**: a spike means the list is wrong for this message. Stop and rethink the segment.
- **AI spend** per lead: if it creeps up, lower the number of pages researched or use a cheaper model for research.
